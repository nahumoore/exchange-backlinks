## What is HARO?

HARO (Help a Reporter Out) matches journalists who need sources with people willing to be quoted, delivered as email digests. Cision shut down the original HARO and tried a paid "Connectively" relaunch in 2024, which users rejected; [Featured.com](https://www.featured.com/) then acquired the brand and relaunched HARO free in April 2025 — three digests a day, 40–100 requests each, back to the original format people used it for.

## Where HARO still wins

A quote picked up by a real publication carries a kind of authority a reciprocal exchange never will — press mentions, brand exposure, and sometimes a much stronger link than anything you'd negotiate directly. If PR reach is the goal, HARO still does something Exchange Backlinks doesn't, and it's worth running alongside a link exchange rather than instead of one.

## Other HARO alternatives for press quotes

If you still want journalist requests rather than a direct link trade, these are the sites like HARO most teams try. Pricing and plans change, so check each one before you commit time to it.

- **[Exchange Backlinks](/submit)** — not a press tool: a free, weekly match with B2B sites in your niche, where the backlink is the deal itself. The best fit when the link, not the quote, is what you're after.
- **[Featured.com](/alternatives/featured)** — the company that now owns HARO. Expert-quote marketplace with free and paid tiers; you answer questions and publishers pick the answers they use.
- **[Help a B2B Writer](/alternatives/help-a-b2b-writer)** — free source requests from writers covering B2B topics, closer to a B2B site's niche than HARO's general press digests.
- **[Qwoted](https://www.qwoted.com/)** — connects reporters with expert sources. There's a free plan with a limited number of pitches, and paid plans for more.
- **[Source of Sources (SOS)](https://sourceofsources.com/)** — a free email digest of journalist requests, started in 2023 by Peter Shankman, the original founder of HARO.
- **[SourceBottle](https://www.sourcebottle.com/)** — free call-outs from journalists, bloggers, and podcasters, with its strongest coverage in Australia and New Zealand.
- **#JournoRequest** — the hashtag journalists use on X to ask for sources. Free and fast-moving, and skewed toward UK media.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is HARO still free?

Yes. After Cision's paid "Connectively" relaunch was rejected by users, Featured.com acquired HARO and brought back the free, email-digest format in April 2025.

### How many HARO requests come through per day?

Three digests a day, each with roughly 40–100 requests across every industry and beat — most of which won't be relevant to a B2B niche site.

### Does answering a HARO request guarantee a backlink?

No. Whether the journalist credits you — and links to you — is entirely their call, and plenty of used quotes go uncredited or unlinked.

### What's the best free HARO alternative for a guaranteed backlink?

Exchange Backlinks — one weekly email matched to your B2B niche, and the link is the deal both sides agreed to, not something you're hoping a journalist grants. Compare it with the rest of the [free alternatives](/alternatives).
